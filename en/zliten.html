      Zliten

      A city of scholarship and coastal heritage

      Home › Cities › Zliten

      Zliten is a historic city on Libya's western coast, roughly 160 kilometres east of Tripoli and close to the Roman ruins of Leptis Magna. Known for centuries as a centre of Islamic scholarship, Zliten is home to one of Libya's most important religious and educational institutions, the zawiya of Sheikh Abdussalam Al-Asmar, and its surroundings preserve Roman-era mosaics that testify to a long and layered history along this stretch of the Mediterranean.

      History of Zliten

      Zliten's location on the coastal trade route between Tripoli and Cyrenaica made it a natural resting point for caravans for centuries, and its name is popularly said to derive from an Arabic phrase referring to the shade of the fig trees once found here, though this origin remains debated. Roman-era remains near the coast, including the mosaic-rich Villa of Omira, point to the area's importance during antiquity, before it developed further under successive Arab, Ottoman, and Italian rule. Zliten's most distinctive historical role has been as a centre of learning: the zawiya founded by Sheikh Abdussalam Al-Asmar has taught the Quran and Maliki jurisprudence for more than 500 years and remains one of the most significant religious institutions in Libya. The city also has a documented Jewish community dating back to the Roman period, revived in the 16th century, whose members largely emigrated by the mid-20th century.

      Top Things to Do in Zliten

      The Zawiya of Sheikh Abdussalam Al-Asmar

      A centuries-old centre of Islamic learning and one of Libya's most historically significant religious institutions, central to Zliten's identity as a city of scholarship.

      Roman mosaics at Villa of Omira

      Coastal Roman-era ruins near Zliten preserve detailed mosaic floors, offering a glimpse of the wealth that once flowed through this part of Tripolitania.

      Al-Asmariya Islamic University

      One of Libya's renowned Islamic universities, carrying forward the city's long tradition of religious and academic study.

      The Mediterranean coastline

      Zliten's stretch of coast, close to both Misrata and the Leptis Magna ruins, offers a quieter alternative to Libya's larger coastal cities.

      Local markets

      Zliten's town markets remain a good place to see everyday Libyan commerce, from produce and spices to traditional crafts tied to the city's long trading history.

      Best Time to Visit & Getting There

      Autumn through spring offers the most comfortable weather for visiting Zliten, avoiding the peak summer heat. The city sits directly on the coastal highway between Tripoli and Misrata, making it an easy stop when travelling east toward Leptis Magna or Misrata, and most visitors combine it with a wider tour of Tripolitania's coastal heritage sites.

      Zliten's blend of scholarship, coastal heritage, and Roman remains make it a worthwhile stop for travellers moving along Libya's western coast between Tripoli and Misrata.
